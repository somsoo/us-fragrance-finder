📖 Olfactory Science: The Fragrance Wheel & Concentrations

Professional perfumery structures scents using standardized taxonomic families and chemical volatility rates:

  • The Fragrance Wheel (Michael Edwards): Formulated in 1983, it classifies scents into four primary families (Floral, Oriental/Amber, Woody, and Fresh). Blends on adjacent spokes share natural chemical accords that harmonize smoothly.
  • Note Volatility Hierarchy: Scent molecules evaporate at different rates based on molecular weight: light monoterpenes (citrus) evaporate rapidly, while heavy sesquiterpenes and resins (sandalwood, amber) anchor the fragrance for hours.
  • Concentration Tiers: Eau de Cologne (EDC: 2-4% oils), Eau de Toilette (EDT: 5-15% oils), Eau de Parfum (EDP: 15-20% oils), and Extrait / Parfum (20-40% oils). Higher concentrations offer longer skin longevity with tighter personal sillage.

Perfumery references: Edwards M. (1983) Fragrances of the World; Turin L. & Sanchez T. (2008) Perfumes: The A-Z Guide.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Fragrance Wheel classification system?

Created by British perfumer Michael Edwards in 1983, the Fragrance Wheel standardizes scents into four primary families: Floral, Amber/Oriental, Woody, and Fresh.

What is the difference between Top, Middle, and Base notes?

Top notes are volatile citrus or herbal scents detected in the first 15 minutes. Heart notes form the core floral or spicy identity for 2 to 4 hours. Base notes provide warm woods and musks lasting 6 to 12 hours.

How does perfume concentration affect performance?

Eau de Toilette (EDT, 5-15% oils) provides higher projection for 3 to 5 hours, whereas Eau de Parfum (EDP, 15-20% oils) and Extraits offer closer sillage with 8 to 14+ hours of longevity.
